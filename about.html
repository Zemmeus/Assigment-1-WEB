About EduSphere

EduSphere started as a study project of three second-year students at Astana IT University. We were tired of courses that end with a certificate and no portfolio, so we designed the platform we wanted to study on ourselves.

Our mission

Make practice the centre of the course, not a bonus

On most platforms a student watches for ten hours and writes code for one. We invert that ratio. Each EduSphere module contains one short theory block and three practical tasks, and no one moves on until the mentor has accepted the work.

Everything you see on this site is written by hand: six HTML pages and one external stylesheet, with no Bootstrap, no Tailwind and no UI library of any kind.

Principles we teach by

  • Structure before decoration — content first, CSS second
  • Small tasks with fast feedback beat long lectures
  • Every student presents work out loud at least once a module
  • Accessibility and semantics are part of the grade, not extras

Our story

From an idea to a published website

  1. March 2025 — the three of us meet in a university web technologies elective.
  2. June 2025 — we run a free two-week HTML workshop for 30 school students.
  3. January 2026 — the first paid cohort of 18 students starts.
  4. September 2026 — the EduSphere website is published on GitHub Pages.
We do not want students to finish a course. We want them to finish a project.

Who studies with us

Three groups of students, three different reasons to enrol.

School students

Grades 9–11

Preparing for university entrance exams and looking for a first real project to put in an application portfolio.

University students

First and second year

Filling the gaps between semesters — most often JavaScript, SQL and version control with Git.

Career switchers

Adults changing profession

Studying in the evening group after work, usually aiming for a junior front-end or analyst position within a year.
